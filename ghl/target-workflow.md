# The target workflow

One journey, five recorded steps. Every inquiry, from any door, ends as a recorded outcome with a named owner.

1. **Website or message** - a patient or an HR team reaches us: form, chat, Facebook or call.
2. **Inquiry record + consent** - saved as a contact with its source. Consent for follow-up is separate from newsletter opt-in.
3. **Approved reply** - we answer only from approved facts. We never quote rates or diagnose.
4. **Staff follow-up** - a named owner plus a backup gets the handoff. Our response clock starts.
5. **Recorded outcome** - booked, quoted, called back or closed. The result is logged.

Some of these steps are still plans. We release each step only when its evidence check passes - we judge on recorded results, not feature lists.

## What "AI marketing" means for us

An AI label is not one thing. We treat three levels as different authority and different risk:

1. **Writing assistance** - draft an approved health update, subject line or social post for a person to review. Lowest risk. We start here.
2. **Workflow automation** - send an approved message after a recorded request or opt-in. Safe with explicit triggers and consent.
3. **Autonomous decisions** - software picks or sends content without review. We do not start here. We make no unreviewed clinical or marketing claims.

We start with human-approved content and explicit triggers. We do not assume an AI label means correct clinical advice, consent compliance, better delivery or more visits. No vendor performance claims or customer testimonials count as CCDI results.

## The fair test for every candidate

Each platform we consider must demonstrate the same chain, on our own site, not a vendor demo:

approved answer → correct lead record and consent → named staff handoff → delivered message → recorded outcome.

For each candidate we also check exact editions, staff access, language behavior, data handling, exports and total operating effort. A broad suite can reduce connections; a connected stack can isolate tools. Neither is automatically better.
